# Research 01 — Competitive Landscape (July 2026)

> Produced 2026-07-25 by the competitors research agent. Part of the Helpee
> pre-build research phase. See docs/RESEARCH.md for the synthesis.

**Headline finding up front:** the most important thing found is not a competitor. It's that (1) every well-capitalized attempt at an *indoor flying home robot* has died or stalled — Ring's Always Home Cam never shipped in 6 years, Samsung shelved Ballie indefinitely in January 2026, Amazon bricked Astro for Business — and (2) roughly a third of global helium supply went offline in March 2026 when Iranian missiles hit Qatar's Ras Laffan complex, doubling spot prices. Both are addressable, but they change what the pitch has to answer.

The second most important finding: **the buyer in this market is a state aging agency, not a consumer.** ElliQ's real business is a $2.1M/3-year NYSOFA contract; Hyodol's is 12,000+ units bought by Korean local governments. That's where proven money is.

## B. The five most important strategic facts

**1. Every serious attempt at an indoor flying home robot has failed to reach market — and the reasons are documented.**
Ring announced the Always Home Cam in **September 2020 at $249.99**, promised 2021 shipping, and as of **September 2025** it remains invite-only with no retail availability. Samsung **indefinitely shelved Ballie in January 2026** after six years and reclassified it as internal research ([Bloomberg, Jan 7 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-07/samsung-s-rolling-ballie-robot-indefinitely-shelved-after-delays)). Amazon **bricked Astro for Business** seven months post-launch and **killed Alexa Together in May 2025**.

Use both edges. Bear case: the hardest problems here defeated Amazon and Samsung with unlimited money. The credible bull case: **all three failures were rotor-based or wheel-based.** Ring's drone had propellers — loud, dangerous near people, downwash; Ballie and Astro had to solve floor navigation in cluttered real homes. Helium sidesteps *precisely* the physics that killed them: no rotor noise, no downwash, intrinsically safe on contact, no floor to navigate. That's a mechanism, not a hope. How you'd know it's working: a 30-day in-home reliability metric nobody else can hit — hours of unattended operation per intervention.

**2. A third of global helium supply went offline in March 2026 and spot prices doubled within days.**
Iranian ballistic missiles struck Qatar's Ras Laffan Industrial City on **March 18–19, 2026**; the shutdown took roughly **one-third of global helium supply** offline and **spot prices doubled within days** ([Exiger](https://www.exiger.com/perspectives/iran-war-disrupts-one-third-of-global-helium-supply/), [gasworld](https://www.gasworld.com/story/update-qatar-helium-2-plant-remains-offline/2247758.article/)). Helium-sealed hard drives rose 20–50%. *Caveat: AGBI framed it as "11% of global supply at risk" ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/industry/2026/03/qatari-lng-shutdown-puts-11-of-global-helium-supply-at-risk/)), so one-third is the high estimate.*

Partial relief has arrived — North American helium **down 9.7% to $50.84/MC in July 2026** — but retail balloon-grade is **$0.45–$0.75/cu ft as of Feb 2026, more than double a decade ago**, and shortages historically trigger rationing of balloon-grade specifically, since it's first deprioritized when MRI and semiconductors compete ([JinHong Gas](https://jh-gas.com/helium-price-trends-2026-supply-challenges-market-demand-and-buying-strategies/)).

Unit economics still work — at 1g lift/liter, a ~300L envelope is ~10.6 cu ft, so **$5–$8 per full fill**. That's not the problem. The problem is *cadence and who refills*: helium diffuses through any practical envelope, and an 85-year-old living alone cannot maintain a gas system. This has to be the base station's job or a mail-in/service model, designed in from day one. It's also the most likely question to sink an investor meeting — have the answer rehearsed.

**3. The proven buyer is a state aging agency, and the proven price is a government contract.**
NYSOFA signed a **$2.1M three-year contract** for ElliQ, acquired ~900 units, deployed ~750 ([NYSOFA update, Feb 2026](https://aging.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/02/nysofa-elliq-project-update-2026.pdf)). Reported outcomes: **95% reduction in loneliness**, **94%** feel less lonely, **97%** feel better overall, active users interacting **~37×/day for ~23 min**. Washington routes ElliQ through Medicaid in-home care; Florida has a program. In Korea, **12,000+ Hyodol units** were bought overwhelmingly by local governments.

Two numbers to hold together: New York's aging office has roughly **$100 per senior** in budget ([Boston Globe, June 28 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/28/opinion/seniors-loneliness-robots-relationship-elliq/)), yet found $2.1M for this. The money exists but only for something that lands as an *outcomes program*, procured via RFP, with a legislative champion. That's a 6–18 month enterprise sale, not a Shopify store.

**4. SafelyYou proved cameras in seniors' bedrooms are sellable — and Sensi.AI proved the opposite sells too.**
SafelyYou raised **$43M Series C** (Jan 2025, >$100M total), is used by **50% of the largest providers**, claims **40% fewer falls, 80% fewer fall-related ER visits** — all on cameras in memory care bedrooms. Simultaneously **Sensi.AI raised $45M** (Oct 2025, $98M total, **400% revenue growth**, **80% of largest NA home care networks**) selling explicitly **"no cameras, no wearables."**

Both work because the buyers differ. Cameras clear the bar in *facilities*, where liability math dominates and consent is institutionalized. In private homes the camera is the objection, and radar/audio wins. Consumer privacy-preserving fall cameras have converged on **~$200 + ~$45/mo**, and the standard workaround is on-device stick-figure extraction so no video leaves the house — adopt that. The bathroom should be a room a floating camera is architecturally incapable of entering.

**5. The care labor shortage is real, worsening in 2026, and is the strongest tailwind.**
Only **19% of US nursing homes currently meet the CMS staffing minimums** mandated for 2029; **~46% have limited new admissions**, 19% closed a wing or floor; CNA turnover **42.34%**; **7 of 10 homes have fewer employees than pre-COVID** ([AHCA/NCAL](https://www.ahcancal.org/News-and-Communications/Blog/Pages/2025%E2%80%932026-Nursing-Home-Report-Reveals-Lower-Turnover.aspx)). **Over 25% of direct care workers are foreign-born**, and 2026 immigration enforcement is deepening the shortage ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/nursing-home-staffing-shortage-immigration-enforcement-2026-care-quality-475916)). Demand side: **~16.2M** community-dwelling older adults live alone (28%, rising to **~42% of women 75+**), **40% of older adults report loneliness** (AARP), Medicare spends an estimated **$6.7B/year** on socially isolated seniors.

## C. Where Helpee genuinely differentiates — and where it doesn't

### Real, defensible differentiation

**Vertical mobility without floor navigation.** Strongest technical claim, and underrated. Every wheeled competitor — Astro, Ballie, temi, the NCT07451223 patrol robot — dies on thresholds, rugs, cords, closed doors, stairs. A blimp at eye level has none of that problem class. One camera covers a whole dwelling by *going* to the person, versus SafelyYou's one-camera-per-room capex. Genuinely different cost structure for whole-home coverage.

**Silence and intrinsic safety.** The Cuddle-Fish research validates this directly: no propellers, no noise, no downwash, and a soft helium body making accidental contact with people, pets, and furniture far safer than a conventional drone ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/video-floating-robot-inspired-by-tinker-bell-and-mew-reimagines-home-companionship)). In a 24-participant study, people **spontaneously patted, stroked, hugged, and cheek-touched** the floating robot with no prompting ([arXiv 2504.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01293)). That's peer-reviewed evidence the form factor produces affective attachment — the thing ElliQ works hard for from a tabletop. Put this paper in the deck.

**Cute-animal framing as privacy affordance.** Tombot's 18,000 preorders at $1,500 and Joy for All's 750,000 adoptions prove the animal frame sells here. A floating creature that comes to you reads differently than a wall camera watching you, even with the same sensor. Soft, but real, and it's the wedge against the camera objection in homes.

**Presence during the event, not just detection of it.** PERS detects and calls. SafelyYou detects and notifies staff. A device that arrives, illuminates, talks to the person on the floor, and stays with them is a different product. Note the nursing-home patrol trial is chasing exactly this — so assume you're racing, not alone.

### Differentiation that is weak or claimed-but-not-real

**"LLM-powered conversation" is table stakes.** Hyodol ships ChatGPT conversation to 12,000 government-purchased dolls. ElliQ has it. Eighteen Chinese companies at CES 2026 showed emotional-AI companions. Not a feature — a dependency.

**"Model-agnostic — owner plugs in their own API key" is a liability, not a differentiator.** No 85-year-old living alone, no adult child mid-caregiving-crisis, and no nursing home procurement officer wants to manage an API key. ElliQ, Hyodol, and care.coach all bundle inference because that's what buyers want. BYO-key is a *developer* selling point in a market with zero developers. Keep model-agnosticism internally as a cost/vendor-risk hedge — genuinely smart architecture — but don't make it customer-facing.

**Camera-based fall detection is a crowded, well-funded fight.** SafelyYou has >$100M and half the largest providers. Radar owns the bathroom, where ~80% of falls happen and where a floating camera can never go. Positioning Helpee as a fall-detection product means competing where it's weakest.

**"Escalates to family" has a fresh corpse.** Amazon built exactly that (activity feed + urgent response + caregiver loop) as Alexa Together and killed it May 2025. PERS charges $35/mo for escalation backed by a **24/7 monitored human call center** — which Helpee won't have at launch. An app notification to a daughter isn't the same product and can't command the same price.

### Honest hard constraints to have answers for

- **Envelope size.** At ~1g lift/liter, 150g payload plus envelope and electronics needs roughly 300–400L — a sphere about **80–90cm across**. Charming as a manatee or whale; not "small."
- **Payload is the named central challenge** in the Keio work; their recommendations are ultra-light drone-grade electronics, offload computation externally, minimize onboard processing. The dumb-aircraft/smart-base-station split is the *correct* answer and matches what researchers independently concluded — say so explicitly, it's a credibility marker.
- **Air currents.** HVAC vents, ceiling fans, opening doors move a neutrally buoyant body you can't fight with 150g of thrust. Needs a real answer.
- **Refill cadence and who refills.** Unsolved in all prior art. Solve it in the architecture.
- **Dementia populations.** NYSOFA **excludes users with cognitive vulnerabilities like Alzheimer's** from the ElliQ program. A floating object near someone with dementia needs its own safety story — memory care is likely a later market, not the first.

## D. Who actually pays, and what price points are proven

Ranked by strength of evidence:

**1. State/county aging agencies + Medicaid waivers — strongest evidence.** NYSOFA's $2.1M/3yr for ~900 units ≈ **$780/unit/year (~$65/mo/senior)**, paid by government. Washington routes ElliQ through Medicaid in-home care; Florida has a program; Medicaid waivers in 14 states cover some eldercare robots. Korean local governments bought 12,000+ Hyodols at **$879–$1,800/unit**. If Helpee has one go-to-market, it's this — meaning the product must be procurable: outcomes data, HIPAA posture, a pilot with a named agency.

**2. Adult children, out of pocket — well-established band.** PERS sets floor and ceiling: **$31.95–$46.95/mo** (Medical Guardian) up to **$49.95–$89.95/mo** (Life Alert, + $197 fee, 3-year contract). ElliQ sits mid-band at **$39.99/mo** + **$249** upfront. Privacy-camera fall detection: **~$200 + ~$45/mo**. care.coach reaches **$199/mo** only because humans are in the loop. **Proven target: $30–50/month recurring with $200–300 upfront.** Above ~$60/mo you need a monitored call center to justify it. Pressure to note: 82% of sandwich-generation caregivers say care costs already strain the household ([Care.com 2026](https://www.care.com/c/sandwich-generation-caregiver-report/)).

**3. Long-term care insurance — real and emerging.** Policies written after 2023 increasingly cover companion robots at **$2,000–$5,000/year**. Highest price point available to a device; worth investigating early.

**4. Facilities — buy on liability and labor, not companionship.** Senior living IT runs **$2,800–$3,500/resident/year (~$233–$292/mo)** covering EHR, security, wireless ([Meriplex](https://meriplex.com/it-budget-per-resident-senior-living/)). A new line item fits in tens of dollars/resident/month, or gets sold the SafelyYou way: 40% fewer falls, 80% fewer ER visits, quantified liability reduction. Scale reference for hard ROI: TUG $105k–$140k or $1,500/mo; Moxi ~$20k+/yr.

**5. Medicare Advantage — do not build the plan on this.** In 2026 only **12% of individual MA plans** (87% of SNPs) offer any SSBCI benefit, and **Medicare does not broadly cover companion or social robots**. PARO has a narrow DME pathway in select circumstances — the argument for eventually pursuing FDA clearance on the fall-detection function, since that's how PARO became reimbursable. Treat MA as a year-3 unlock.

## E. Has anyone tried this exact idea?

**No one has ever brought an indoor companion blimp to commercial sale.** Four categories of prior attempt, all short of a shipped consumer product:

**Research — very close to the concept, very recently.** **Cuddle-Fish**, led by Mingyang Xu at Keio University's Graduate School of Media Design with collaborators including MIT Media Lab: helium-filled soft robot, carbon-fiber flapping wings, movable internal mass for pitch/altitude, microcontroller, 3.7V Li-Po. Hovers silently for hours. Demo scenarios are **reminder tool, alarm clock, study companion, dance partner** — overlapping Helpee almost exactly. Published at Augmented Humans 2025 ([arXiv 2504.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01293)), followed by a full design framework, "Floating Companion: Exploring Design Space for Soft Floating Robots in Indoor Environments," at **DIS 2026** ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800645.3813051)), mapping ~10 design dimensions. Heavy recent coverage (Digital Trends, Interesting Engineering, DroneXL, Khaleej Times — all July 2026) plus a TEDx talk. **Explicitly not near commercialization; no startup or spin-off found.** Also relevant: Georgia Tech's **GT-MAB** miniature autonomous blimp already demonstrated monocular-camera **face detection, person following, and gesture recognition** — Helpee's exact perception stack, on a blimp, already proven in a lab.

**Product attempt, non-senior:** **Yuling Infinite's BOOBOO**, a flying bionic flapping-wing emotional companion at **CES 2026**. No price, no ship date, not eldercare. The only company actively trying to productize a flying companion.

**Adjacent commercial blimps that pivoted away from the home:** **Aerotain** (ETH Zurich spinoff, Sept 2015) sold Skye for events/advertising — 90+ events, later "Aerotain Mini" four times smaller for low ceilings — but no news after ~2020 and no consumer product; treat as dormant. **NTT Docomo** built a bladeless ultrasonic-propulsion balloon drone with LED envelope targeting sale by **March 2022**, aimed at events and commercial facilities; no evidence it became a consumer product. **Spacial** (Brooklyn, ~2015–2017) sold the **Halo**, an indoor helium blimp for filming and indoor arenas; no evidence of a surviving business.

**What this means.** There's no incumbent to displace and no cautionary corpse of this specific idea — genuinely rare and genuinely good. But not because nobody thought of it: Keio and MIT are actively publishing, Georgia Tech proved the perception stack, and a Chinese startup showed a flying companion six months ago. The moat isn't the idea. It's the boring parts: helium logistics, the base-station split, a monitored escalation path, and a state aging agency willing to sign an RFP. Notably, **nobody has combined the floating form factor with the safety/escalation layer and the government-procurement channel** — and that combination, not the blimp, is the actual product.
